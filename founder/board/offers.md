# Offers lens

Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

**Market pain is real and reachable.** Engaged couples (25–45, in Hungary) face a genuine problem: they want professional wedding materials but don't want to hire a designer or use impersonal tools like Canva. The pain is documented (wedding blogs, Pinterest searches, forums). They exist in defined, searchable channels.

**Time-to-outcome is excellent.** The app delivers results in minutes, not weeks. A couple with a vision can have print-ready files same-day. This is a sharp advantage over commissioning a designer.

**The product is real and live.** You have a working v6.47 with a complete feature set (WYSIWYG editor, templates, print CSS, multi-language). You can ship improvements and gather customer feedback in weeks, not months.

**Budget exists.** Engaged couples planning weddings spend €5k–€50k+ total. A €17 PDF and €3–€5 per printed copy is a rounding error in that budget. The weddings-per-year market (thousands in Hungary alone) is mature and reliable.

---

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

**1. You have no evidence the market is actually buying.** The brief says pricing is "not validated against customer willingness-to-pay" and you don't know current user numbers, repeat rate, or actual AOV. If couples aren't discovering you, converting, or re-using the app, the market may not actually want this offer. Without organic demand proof (emails, sign-ups, word-of-mouth velocity), Hormozi would say this fails market selection. A bad market is the #1 reason offers fail.

**2. Weak likelihood: no proof it works.** The offer asks couples to trust they'll get a result they love without portfolio examples, testimonials, or guarantees. Couples haven't seen "this is what real weddings used us for" or "here are 50 real examples." Hormozi emphasizes: buyers won't believe you without proof. A money-back guarantee and a visible portfolio of real weddings are non-negotiable.

**3. Commodity price trap.** At €17 for a digital PDF, you're competing on price, not value. Canva (free), template designers on Etsy (€5–€20), and cheap freelancers online all undercut or match you. If the only difference is "faster than hiring a designer," that's fragile—the market might choose cheap Canva templates instead. The offer needs to answer: "Why is *this* the obvious choice, not just the cheapest?"

---

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. **How many engaged couples have actually used this, paid, and got a wedding magazine printed?** If you don't know, measure it this week. Hormozi asks: who exactly has the pain and the money? You need to know how many real customers you have and whether they're repeating.

2. **What stops someone from using Canva or hiring a €50 designer instead?** What is so much better about your offer that a couple says "I'd be stupid not to use this"? Right now, the answer isn't clear.

3. **Why should a couple believe they'll love the result before they buy?** Do you have 50+ real wedding magazine examples on your site? Customer testimonials? A guarantee ("Love it or refunded")? Without these, the likelihood score is low.

4. **What is your actual cost to print and deliver?** If printing costs you €2 per copy and you're selling at €3–€5, what's your margin? If print quality or delivery is bad, that kills word-of-mouth.

5. **Could you charge 50,000 HUF (€170) instead of 5,000?** If couples are avoiding hiring a designer (which costs €500–€2,000), are you capturing the value, or leaving it on the table? Hormozi asks: could you double the price and still be the obvious choice?

6. **Who is your first 100 customers and how did they arrive?** Organic search? Referral? Paid ads? If you don't know, you don't have a repeatable acquisition model yet.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Proof of market demand:** Show 50+ real customers who have bought and used the product, with repeat rate and net revenue data. If you have fewer than 20 paid customers, the market is not yet validating the offer.

2. **Portfolio and social proof:** Build a visible gallery of 30+ real wedding magazines created by customers, with 1–2 customer testimonials. Publish the best examples on your home page.

3. **Guarantee:** Offer "Love it or full refund within 7 days." This removes risk for the buyer and tests whether you actually have product-market fit. If refund rate is <5%, you're onto something.

4. **Value stack:** Add bonuses that solve adjacent problems:
   - Matching digital invitation (email-able version)
   - QR code to wedding website or registry (one-click)
   - Print files optimized for local Hungarian print shops (reduce delivery friction)
   - Option for a designer to touch-up the layout (+€50–€100)
   
   These are cheap to deliver and raise the perception of value.

5. **Price test:** Increase digital PDF to 15,000–20,000 HUF (€50–€67) and print to 2,000 HUF (€6.70) per copy. Run this for 2 weeks. If conversions don't drop and revenue stays flat or grows, you've found the real price. If conversions crater, you'll know the old price was the market's limit.

6. **Marketing model:** Identify your fastest, cheapest acquisition channel (Pinterest? wedding Facebook groups? wedding blogs?). Before you fund, run a 2-week test with 500 HUF spend and show 3+ organic sign-ups with 1+ conversion. Then you know how to scale.

If you hit these, the offer moves from "interesting idea with a real market" to "proven product people are buying and using." Then it's fundable.
