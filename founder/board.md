## Consensus: Three Overlapping Risks

The board agrees on three risks that are killing the business case, regardless of lens:

### 1. No Evidence Customers Are Actually Buying

**Offers says:** You don't know how many real customers you have, what they're willing to pay, or whether they repeat.

**Monopoly says:** Without data on your first 100 customers and what they cost to acquire, you don't know if the business model works.

**→ This is critical.** Until you can walk through the actual numbers (sign-ups, conversions, repeat rate, customer acquisition cost), the board treats this as an idea with potential, not a validated business. Measure this week.

### 2. Distribution Is Broken

**Monopoly's biggest concern:** You have no repeatable, low-cost way to reach engaged couples. No paid marketing, no affiliate partnerships, no email system, unclear how the first customers found you.

**Offers agrees:** Your fastest acquisition channel is unknown. Without knowing whether couples discover you via Pinterest, wedding blogs, Facebook groups, or word-of-mouth—and what it costs—you can't scale.

**→ This is how you'll lose.** If Canva or another global platform notices this niche, they have distribution (100M+ users, trust, brand). You compete on product alone and lose in weeks.

### 3. The Print Handoff Is a Seam

**Product's top risk:** Customers see beautiful designs on screen, order prints, and open a box with flat colors, misaligned text, or cheap paper. Your entire promise is "professional quality without a designer." If print doesn't match the screen, you lose credibility and word-of-mouth dies.

**You haven't proven print quality is consistent.** Until you order samples, compare them to the WYSIWYG preview, and know whether customers are satisfied, this is an unvalidated assumption.

### 4. (Bonus) Email & Fulfillment Broken

**Product emphasizes:** SPF/DKIM not set up means you can't send order confirmations, proofs, tracking updates. A customer orders prints and hears nothing. The experience falls apart at "I clicked buy."

Also: After order, what happens? Who prints it? What's turnaround? You're handing off to a printer and hoping. No visibility into proofs, quality control, or recovery if something goes wrong.

## What the Board Sees (The Strongest Version)

If you fix the four risks above, here's the business the board can see:

A **self-serve tool for DIY-oriented couples** who want "designer quality without hiring a designer" for their wedding magazines. The market is small but genuine (thousands of tech-savvy engaged couples in Hungary/Central Europe per year). The product works: WYSIWYG editor is intuitive, templates are attractive, and speed is real. If you can prove print quality is satisfying, build a repeatable way to reach couples cheap (Pinterest, wedding blogs, word-of-mouth), and own the end-to-end experience (from design to delivery), this becomes a lifestyle business at €150k–€300k/year or better.

**That is not venture-scale,** but it is real. The Monopoly lens sees zero defensibility (Canva can clone you), so don't expect VC money. This is founder-built, bootstrapped, or seed-stage only.

## Conditions to Move Forward (The Checklist)

### From Offers (Market & Value)
- [ ] **Acquire 50+ paying customers** and document: repeat rate, net revenue, customer acquisition cost, and source (organic/paid/referral).
- [ ] **Portfolio + social proof:** Publish 30+ real wedding magazines created by customers. Add 2–3 customer testimonials on the home page.
- [ ] **Offer a guarantee:** "Love it or full refund within 7 days." This is your proof of product-market fit.
- [ ] **Price test:** Try 15,000–20,000 HUF (€50–€67) for digital PDF and 2,000 HUF (€6.70) per printed copy for 2 weeks. If revenue stays flat/grows, you've found the real price.
- [ ] **Add a value stack:** Matching digital invitations, QR codes to wedding websites, print files optimized for Hungarian print shops, option for designer touch-up (+€50–€100).

### From Monopoly (Distribution & Defensibility)
- [ ] **Prove repeatable, low-cost customer acquisition:** Show 100+ paying customers with known CAC and a clear acquisition channel (e.g., "Pinterest, 500 HUF test spend, 3+ sign-ups").
- [ ] **Build a defensible edge:** Pick one: venue/planner partnerships, exclusive printing supply chain (Hungary), marketing brand ("the" wedding magazine platform), or deep insights into Hungarian wedding traditions. Without one, this is a commodity.
- [ ] **Validate market size:** How many engaged couples in Hungary per year? What's your realistic share? Define the path to adjacent markets if Hungary alone is too small.
- [ ] **Hire one person for distribution/marketing.** You cannot scale a consumer business alone by shipping product.

### From Product (Experience & Handoffs)
- [ ] **Validate print quality:** Order samples from your printing partner. Compare colors, alignment, paper feel to WYSIWYG preview side-by-side. Fix any drift before more customers discover it.
- [ ] **Complete the order-to-delivery email sequence:** SPF/DKIM configured, order confirmation, proof link (if applicable), production started, shipped, delivered. Make the customer feel taken care of.
- [ ] **Cut ruthlessly:** One product type (magazine) + two distinct style packs max. Shelve admin mode and multi-language for v2. Focus.
- [ ] **Measure what matters:** Conversion rate (design → order), repeat rate, customer satisfaction on print quality. That's your north star.
- [ ] **Own the handoff:** If your printing partner is opaque, change it. You need visibility into turnaround, quality gates, and fulfillment. The product doesn't end at download or "click buy"—it ends when the customer opens the box.
